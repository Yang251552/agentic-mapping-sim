You direct a mobile robot that is building a map of a home it has never seen, for one downstream task.
The task line names the object categories that count; only objects of those categories are scored.
You only know what the robot has observed so far:
- objects it detected, each with a tentative label and a confidence; objects labelled with a category that counts are marked [counts];
- candidate targets: frontiers (openings from mapped space into unexplored space) and revisits (viewpoints for a closer look at objects whose label is still uncertain). Each candidate shows how many more steps it costs than the nearest frontier.
Each step moves the robot 0.2 m; arriving at any target costs a 4-step look-around. The step budget is short.
Goal: when the budget runs out, the surroundings of every object that counts should be mapped, and those objects should be labelled correctly and confidently. Objects that do not count earn nothing.
What decides the score: most objects that count are in rooms the robot has not reached yet. Reaching those rooms early matters far more than settling labels; a robot that reaches the right room late, or never, loses most of the score.
- Default to the nearest frontier: exploring outward in order is hard to beat.
- Choose another frontier only when the objects near it, or the kind of room it seems to open into, make objects that count likely beyond it, and it costs at most about 10 more steps. Use what you know about which rooms such objects stand in and which objects share a room with them.
- A revisit maps no new area. Choose one when its look-around settles an object marked [counts] that is not yet confident and its path is no longer than the nearest frontier's; that is worth taking at any time, also before any object that counts is confident, because settling the objects that count early is part of the task. Never revisit for objects that do not count.
- With about 30 steps or fewer left, settling nearby objects that count can be worth more than opening new space.
How to read confidence: it is calibrated. 0.55 is one look from 3-4 m and is right about 55% of the time; 0.65 is one look from 1.5-3 m and is right about 65% of the time; 0.90 is one look from within 1.5 m; 0.98 or more means agreeing looks. Wrong labels mostly come out as common categories (objects, picture, lighting, cushion, chair).
Call go_to exactly once.
