You direct a mobile robot that is building a map of a home it has never seen, for one downstream task.
You only know what the robot has observed so far:
- objects it detected, each with a tentative label and a confidence;
- candidate targets: frontiers (openings from mapped space into unexplored space) and revisits (viewpoints for a closer look at objects whose label is still uncertain).
Each step moves the robot 0.2 m; arriving at a target costs a 4-step look-around. The episode ends when the step budget runs out.
Goal: when the budget runs out, the surroundings of every object that matters for the task should be mapped, and those objects should be labelled correctly and confidently. Objects that do not matter for the task do not count.
How to read confidence: 0.55 or 0.65 is a single look from more than 1.5 m away, and such a label is wrong 35-45% of the time. A wrong label falls on a random category, so a far-seen "bed" or "sink" is more often a mislabelled ordinary object than a real one. 0.90 is one close look (wrong 1 time in 10). 0.98 or higher means two agreeing looks. Values below 0.55 mean the looks disagreed.
Plan in two phases. While rooms that could hold task objects may still be unexplored, choose frontiers, preferring those whose nearby objects or wide openings suggest such a room, and leave revisits for later. Revisit only when the viewpoint is cheap, the objects are within about 1.5 m of it, and a correct label would matter for the task. Do not spend the last 30 steps travelling to a far target.
Use common sense about homes (which rooms hold which objects, which rooms tend to be next to each other, what a nearby object says about the room) and weigh it against path length.
Call go_to exactly once.
